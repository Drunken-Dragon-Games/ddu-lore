# Forge Norsund through history, not ornament

Expert conlangers build a franchise language in a settled order. They choose the purpose first, then build a sound inventory in natural series, then the phonotactics, a seed lexicon, the core grammar, and finally the script, romanization and translation tests. They make it feel real by simulating history, not by stacking exotic features. A proto-language is pushed through ordered, regular sound changes, then through grammaticalization, analogy and dated loan layers, so that irregular forms and dialects come out as leftovers of that history rather than decoration. For Norsund this points to one design stance: **Norse-flavored by process, not by vocabulary**. That means umlaut, breaking, a nominative *-r*, ablaut verbs, a suffixed article and kenning poetry applied to invented roots. Taking real Old Norse words and bending them is what David Peterson calls a throwback to 18th- and 19th-century exoticism, and what critics fault in Robert Jordan's "bland" Old Tongue. DDU is not starting from zero. Its own lore records already contain roughly twenty Norsund-looking forms (*Thiolden* "land of the brave", *Vilkyr/Vilkyren* "free sword(s)", *Nimkjold* "night guard", *Kjoldmur*, *Lysmar*, *Starifjolden*, *Sundwanderen*). Every major screen language, from Klingon and Dothraki to High Valyrian and Na'vi, was reverse-engineered from a starting set that small or smaller. The setting's timeline (settlers around 450 b.v.e., imperial invasion in 37 a.v.e.) argues for a moderate time depth and a thin layer of Kedre borrowings in war, law, church and learning. Auristar's canal-city speech fits as the innovative, Venetian-styled prestige dialect, and a featural "star script" fits as the deliberate opposite of the forbidden runes. The hardest problems are institutional, not linguistic. The records already show the kind of spelling drift (*Midelmar/Middlemar*, *Volggan/Vloggan*) that outran Skyrim's word database. No known conlang decides its canon by direct contributor or token vote. And because no court has ruled that a language can be copyrighted, "official Norsund" can only be a versioned compliance status, backed by open data licensing and trademarks rather than ownership.

## Naturalism comes from simulated history, not invented quirks

The order of operations matters more than any single feature choice. Mark Rosenfelder's *Language Construction Kit* (LCK), still the canonical free starting point, prescribes six steps: decide the sounds, create the lexicon, create the grammar, design an alphabet, design a cursive, translate a text. He warns that "the order of the steps above is significant" and that working backwards "will lead to an inconsistent if not incoherent work." His cautionary case is Hergé's piecemeal Syldavian, from which no consistent phonology can be recovered ([Rosenfelder](https://zompist.com/kit.html)). The newest textbook, Jessie Peterson's *How to Create a Language: The Conlang Guide* (Cambridge University Press, 2025), refines that order in a telling way. **Sound change is taught before basic vocabulary**, and inflection is taught together with grammaticalization so that learners "think of an inflection as an evolutionary process from the very beginning" ([Peterson, FL-00010C-00](https://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/fl-00010C-00.pdf)). David Peterson's companion essay gives the logic ("if the phonology of a language is its ingredients, phonotactics is its recipe") and the caveat: a language "has no beginning and no end," so the builder starts in the middle and accepts constant revision (same source). Rosenfelder's practical drafting order runs from inventory tables through derivation, basic paradigms, adpositions and word order to at least three sample texts and about 1,000 words. He recommends building a **protolanguage first** and deriving the target language from it with a sound-change applier ([Rosenfelder, kitwrite](https://zompist.com/kitwrite.html)).

The naming language is the recognized seed. Jeffrey Henning coined the term in 1995 for "a phonology coupled with rules for compounding". He showed that "with just 150 words… you can generate millions of names," and that the only grammatical decision strictly required is whether a modifier precedes or follows its head ([Henning, FL-00003E-00](http://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/fl-00003E-00.pdf)). David Peterson's version adds a phoneme list, the allowed syllable shapes, word-assembly patterns and **headedness**. Its payoff is forward compatibility: the choices "constrain the possible grammar," so "if the book is optioned for a movie, and a language creator is hired to fill in the blanks later, they won't be tearing their hair out" ([Peterson, "Names Aren't Neutral"](https://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/fl-00005A-00.pdf)). Rosenfelder adds that derivational morphology is worth starting "even for a naming language" ([Rosenfelder, kitwrite](https://zompist.com/kitwrite.html)). Norsund is a common tongue that songs, inscriptions, oaths and games will need in full sentences, so it needs the full-language path. Even so, a frozen naming layer plus a phonotactic validator is the right first deliverable. The minimum that supports dialogue is phoneme tables, phonotactics, basic noun, verb and pronoun paradigms, adpositions, phrase and clause order, a question and negation strategy, a few hundred words and three sample texts.

"Naturalistic" has a concrete meaning. Rosenfelder: "The key to a naturalistic language, in fact, is to add (or subtract) entire dimensions." Sounds come in series, are "not equiprobably distributed," and some phonemes "can only be explained historically." Sound change is "almost completely regular," and naturalistic languages are "full of irregularities, quirky lexical derivations, and interesting idioms" ([Rosenfelder, LCK](https://zompist.com/kitlong.html)). The *a priori*/*a posteriori* line (inventing from scratch versus deriving from a real language, with the "relex" as the crudest form of the latter) is blurry ([UCL PLIN0068](https://www.homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~ucjtudo/assets/teaching/PLIN0068/1_conlang.html)). For fantasy, though, the experts converge. Henning suggested deriving from a real ancestor to save time. David Peterson rejects altering "some specific language… to give it some kind of 'aesthetic'" because "in fantasy it smacks of the old 18th and 19th century exoticism." He offers a test: "If an author finds themself saying 'I think I've altered this language enough that speakers of said language won't be offended', it's a sure sign that the author should be doing something different" ([Peterson](https://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/fl-00005A-00.pdf)). On the Zompist Bulletin Board, posters advise blending at most two or three natural languages so "a few key features can stand out." They dismiss Jordan's Old Tongue, "about half of each" of several sources, as never "either coherent or distinctive" ([verduria.org](https://verduria.org/viewtopic.php?p=7491)). The position this report takes is that **Norsund's roots should be invented and only its typology borrowed**: an invented Proto-Norsund with Germanic-like structure, run through regular change.

David Peterson names the highest level of naturalism, and it is unusually reachable for DDU. Twenty sound changes do not happen in one generation, so "a truly masterful naturalistic conlang will keep careful track of exactly when vocabulary is created in the history of the language and note which processes apply and which don't." He calls this his own "greatest weakness" ([Peterson, FL-00010C-00](https://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/fl-00010C-00.pdf)). DDU's lore already lives in a dated, version-controlled timeline. Tagging every lexeme with the epoch it entered the language therefore costs almost nothing, and it is the same field the governance pipeline needs for provenance (see the final section).

Validation means translation. Rosenfelder tells builders to "write at least three sample texts to find gaps," preferably ones that showcase the culture: a conversation, a proverb collection, a letter ([Rosenfelder, kitwrite](https://zompist.com/kitwrite.html)). The Babel text, which Henning proposed in 1996, and "The North Wind and the Sun" give comparability with other conlangs ([Wikipedia: Babel text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babel_text)). *The Conlang Guide* closes with a translation chapter because translation is "the bulk" of conlang work and "requires entirely different skills" ([Peterson](https://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/fl-00010C-00.pdf)). Examples should be glossed under the **Leipzig Glossing Rules**: ten rules covering hyphenated morphemes, "=" for clitics, "." for one-to-many correspondences and small-caps category labels. Their key principle is that "glosses are part of the analysis, not part of the data" ([MPI-EVA](https://www.eva.mpg.de/lingua/resources/glossing-rules.php)). Rosenfelder's grammar outline (introduction, phonology, morphology, derivation, syntax, semantic fields and pragmatics, writing system, examples, lexicon), with searchable placeholders such as "STD" or "$$$", is the right skeleton for a Norsund reference grammar ([Rosenfelder, kitwrite](https://zompist.com/kitwrite.html)). A Norsund test suite suggests itself: a Vilkyr's oath, a dedication carved on the Kjoldmur, a canal-market haggle, a kenning-laden praise verse by a Sundwanderen bard, and a 50-name generation run checked against the phonotactics.

## Germanic processes on invented roots make Norsund sound Norse

Build the inventory in series, keep it restrained, and let the phonotactics do the characterizing. David Peterson: "sounds come in bunches." It would be "a little odd to have /p, t, k/ and then /b/ and /ɡ/, but not /d/." He suggests a starter set of /p t k s m n l h/ and /i u a/, then expanding ([Peterson](https://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/fl-00005A-00.pdf)). Henning advises against "too many unusual sounds," because "simply having regular sounds combined in unique ways… will be enough." His 1995 typology figures are dated, so use WALS for current counts: 5 to 7 vowels is typical, and consonant inventories average about 22.8 ([Henning](http://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/fl-00003E-00.pdf)). WALS itself, the standard check on whether a feature is common or rare, is a finished, static dataset (v2020.6, CC BY 4.0) ([WALS](https://wals.info/)). Rosenfelder is emphatic that phonotactic constraints "go a long, long way to giving it its own distinctive flavor" ([Rosenfelder, LCK](https://zompist.com/kitlong.html)). The working method for Norsund is to pull WALS values for Icelandic, Norwegian, Swedish, Danish and German as a "Germanic-feeling" baseline. Then deviate on purpose in one to three places, each one confirmed as attested in WALS, so that Norsund is distinct and not a cipher.

The Norse "feel" is a cluster of features. Some are structural, so readers sense them without spotting a source language. Others are recognition triggers that tip into costume when overused.

| Feature | Real Old Norse evidence | Norsund guidance |
|---|---|---|
| i-umlaut | *menn* "men" < \**manniz* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse)) | Lean on it; generate plurals and causatives from trigger suffixes |
| u-umlaut | *lǫnd* "lands" < \**landu* | Use as a dialect marker (kept in insular speech, lost on the mainland) |
| Breaking | *skjǫldr* < \**skelduz* produces *skj-, gj-, hj-* onsets | Lean on it; the canon already has *Kjoldmur*, *Nimkjold*, *Starifjolden* |
| Nominative *-r*, assimilating to *-ll/-nn* | *ulfr* < \**wulfaz*; *Óðinn* < *Óðinʀ* | Lean on it as a masculine-stem ending |
| Ablaut (strong) verbs | Seven classes: *rísa/reis* … *fara/fór* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse_morphology)) | Lean on it; let sound change blur the classes |
| Suffixed article, mediopassive | *hundr-inn* "the hound"; *kalla-sk* from reflexive *sik* | Grammaticalize both from native Norsund words |
| Patronymics | Genitive + *-son/-dóttir*; Icelandic *-bur* "child of" for nonbinary people since 2019 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_name)) | Copy the structure, not the words |
| Special letters | Normalized þ ð æ œ ø ǫ plus acute length marks | Sparingly: two or three at most, and only in an archaic spelling layer |
| Real roots and god names | *-heim*, *Týr* | Sparingly; existing canon is grandfathered, new coinages avoid them |

Irregularity should be generated, not sprinkled. **Sturtevant's paradox** sums up the mechanism: "sound change is regular but produces irregularity, while analogy is irregular but produces regularity" ([Wikipedia: Analogical change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analogical_change)). Old Norse shows it plainly. *Hamarr* "hammer" has plural *hamrar* and dative plural *hǫmrum*, the result of syncope plus u-umlaut. Its seven strong-verb classes partly merged (*bera/bar* and *gefa/gaf*). Some verbs ended up as analogical hybrids, strong verbs with weak pasts (*snúa*, *slá*) ([Wikipedia: Old Norse morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse_morphology)). The recipe for Norsund follows from this. Give Proto-Norsund regular suffixes that trigger umlaut (a plural *-i*, a causative *-ja-*). Apply umlaut, then delete the trigger through final-vowel loss. Later, level most nouns onto a productive plural but exempt a short list of high-frequency words (body parts, kin terms, "star," "boat," "oar"). Frequent words are both the most exposed to sound change and the most resistant to leveling, and grammar can block a change: Brazilian Portuguese drops final /r/ in infinitives but not in nouns ([Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Conlang/Intermediate/History/Common_sound_changes)). A per-word change log (proto-form, each stage, leveled or not, and why) keeps irregularity a documented decision instead of noise. Rosenfelder flags the opposite failure: regularizing paradigms is tempting but unrealistic ([Rosenfelder, kitgram](https://zompist.com/kitgram.html)).

Grammaticalization gives Norsund and Kedre recognizably different histories. The cline runs from content word to grammatical word to clitic to affix, with meaning bleaching and sound wearing away along the way ([Wikipedia: Grammaticalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammaticalization)). Norse turned a demonstrative into the suffixed article and the reflexive *sik* into the mediopassive *-sk*. Romance turned Latin *cantare habeo* ("I have to sing") into a synthetic future (same sources). If Norsund grammaticalizes a native demonstrative and reflexive while Kedre builds a "have"-future, the two languages diverge in structure, not just in sound. On syntax, Germanic languages are fusional, with case and gender. Rosenfelder defends fusion as "compact" and uses German main-clause versus subordinate-clause order to show that mixed word orders are normal ([Rosenfelder, kitgram](https://zompist.com/kitgram.html)). That makes a verb-second main clause a natural candidate for one of Norsund's deliberate Germanic signatures.

The pitfalls to police are well documented. **Relexification** is English grammar with swapped words: without exposure to other languages "you're pretty much doomed to produce ciphers of English." Rosenfelder's remedies are to give every word two English glosses, carve semantic fields your own way, and treat English idioms as idioms ([Rosenfelder, LCK](https://zompist.com/kitlong.html)). The **kitchen sink** is complexity "for no good reason," and ordinary features that do not fit together count ([Linguifex, snippet](https://linguifex.com/wiki/Kitchen_sink_language)). Matt Shields' counter is "THINK SIMPLE… Choose fewer building blocks and be more creative with them" ([Make A Lang](https://makealang.blogspot.com/2007/11/common-conlanging-pitfall.html)). The **Englerika** trap is decorating unrelated stems with *-heim*, *-gard* and *-son* to make them look Viking ([Peterson](https://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/fl-00005A-00.pdf)).

## Thiolden's canon already holds a half-built morpheme bank

Back-fitting existing words is the industry norm. Marc Okrand built Klingon around the handful of lines already spoken in *Star Trek: The Motion Picture* ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/1739817/star-trek-klingon-language-creator-marc-okrand)). David Peterson built Dothraki from about 50 words in George R. R. Martin's books plus Martin's descriptions of how it sounded ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_J._Peterson)). High Valyrian grew from two sentences: *valar morghulis* and *valar dohaeris* became the basis of its verb conjugation, while *dracarys* was treated as an independent word ([Wikipedia: Valyrian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valyrian_languages)). Paul Frommer built Na'vi around about a dozen words James Cameron had already coined ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Na%CA%BCvi_language)). Tolkien worked "both backward and forward, to create 'fitting names'" ([Wikipedia: The Etymologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Etymologies_(Tolkien))). When he reorganized his languages, Helge Fauskanger argues, he reshaped Noldorin into Sindarin partly to keep the established Beleriand names "more or less unchanged" ([Ardalambion](https://www.uib.no/People/hnohf/ilkorin.htm)). The method is consistent across all of them: freeze the canon forms, segment them, assign meanings that fit the lore, design phonology and sound changes that produce them, and file the leftovers as loans, archaisms or words from other languages.

DDU's records already contain such a corpus, and it shows emerging regularities. The segmentations below are this report's proposals, not canon.

| Attested form (lore records) | Stated meaning or use | Candidate analysis | Decision needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Thiolden*, *Thiolnay* | "land of the brave"; demonym | *thiol* "brave" + *den* "land"; *-nay* demonym | *Dethiol* (a Kullmyr leader) also contains *thiol*. Coincidence in another tongue, or a derivation? |
| *Vilkyr*, *Vilkyren* | "Free Sword" in Old Norsund; plural | *vil* "free" + *kyr* "sword"; *-en* plural | *Kyr* also appears as a given name (*Kyr Thor'Vale*), a byname-turned-name pattern |
| *Nimkjold*, *Kjoldmur* | night guard; Auristar's great wall | *nim* "night", *kjold* "guard/shield", *mur* "wall" | *mur* matches Latin *murus*, so it is a candidate early Kedre loan |
| *Sundwanderen* | bards | *sund* + *wander* + *-en* | *sund* recurs in *Norsund*: "song/speech", making Norsund "north-speech"? *wander* is English |
| *Vilnaya*, *Vilnay*, *Vilnayan* | the free towns; demonym; adjective | *vil* + *-nay* | Three spellings need a lemma and derivation rule |
| *Auristar*, *Aurinay* | the fallen star and its city; demonym | Latinate *auri-* + *star(i)* | A Kedre-Norsund hybrid, like English *Grimston* |
| *Starifjolden*, *Starirauggheim* | cosmic caverns; temple tower | *stari* "star" + *fjold-*; + *raugg* + *heim* | Define *fjold* and *raugg* |
| *Lysmar*, *Midelmar* | Norsund name of the Kedre goddess Luxis; central mountain | *lys* "light" + *mar*; *midel* "middle" + *mar* | Is *mar* one morpheme or two? *Midelmar* is also spelled *Middlemar* |
| *Marheim*, *Oudeheim*, *Farheim* | lost city; its later name; settlers' home island | X + *heim* "home" | *oude* is Dutch for "old", and *-heim* is a real Germanic element |
| *Kullmyr*, *Nurmyr*, *Runmyren* | peoples and groups | X + *myr* "folk" (+ *-en*) | Is *-myren* the plural? |

The same records show the hazards a back-fit has to settle. There is spelling drift in *Midelmar/Middlemar*, *Volggan/Vloggan*, *Haakon/Hakkon* and *Varian/Vairean*. *Valinor*, Tolkien's name for the Undying Lands, labels a region in `records/varian-empire.yaml`, and it is the most urgent rename. Apostrophes appear in *Thor'Vale*, *Karhak'Modan* and *Ik'nal'tar*. Real Norse personal names (*Astrid*, *Haakon*, *Ulf*) and a god's name (*Tyr*) are in use, and English-transparent names include *Winterbeard* and *Wintermare*. None of this requires renaming canon wholesale. Henning notes that real place names "vary idiosyncratically and do not always evolve according to the regular sound changes": *Luton* and *Leyton* both descend from *Lygetun* ([Henning](http://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/fl-00003E-00.pdf)). Okrand's stance was that anything already spoken "counts" ([StarTrek.com](https://www.startrek.com/news/qapla-klingon-language-creator-marc-okrand-part-2)). Legacy forms can therefore be grandfathered as irregular, archaic or foreign, while new coinages follow the rules. English-transparent bynames such as *Winterbeard* are best declared a translation convention, the way saga translations render "Eirik Ale-Lover" ([Ruiter, Aberdeen](https://www.abdn.ac.uk/news/8745/)).

Lexicon size has well-calibrated targets. The Swadesh 207 list is the most widely used seed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swadesh_list)). The **Leipzig–Jakarta list** (2009) holds the 100 concepts that were most widespread and **most resistant to borrowing** across 41 languages, led by "fire," "nose," "to go" and "water." Only 62 of its items overlap with Swadesh-100 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig%E2%80%93Jakarta_list)). That makes it a ready-made "never a Kedre loan" list for Norsund. Rosenfelder's *Conlanger's Lexipedia* adds a 1,500-item Fantasy Frequency Wordlist drawn from a 1.1-million-word corpus of fantasy and science fiction, "designed for prioritizing vocabulary work" ([zompist.com](https://www.zompist.com/lexipedia.html)). Franchise benchmarks are about 1,700 Dothraki words delivered before shooting, rising to 3,163 by September 2011 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dothraki_language)). Na'vi had about 500 words built for the film's dialogue and about 1,000 at its 2009 release, and passed 2,600 by 2020 after a community expansion project grew it by more than half ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Na%CA%BCvi_language)). Headline counts conflict: Nautilus credits Dothraki with about 10,000 words, which looks like a goal rather than a count ([Nautilus](https://lede-v2.nautil.us/14-words-for-horse-the-linguistics-of-game-of-thrones-234392)). Track root counts separately from headwords. The experts favor few roots and much derivation. Rosenfelder found *stand* underlying at least forty derivations ([zompist.com](https://www.zompist.com/lexipedia.html)). Peterson builds "word-nets" in which Irathient words from "breeze" to "storm" to "goodbye" to "destiny" share a single root. He also warns that the hardest words are the most basic, like "turn" and "twist," because their dozens of English senses "never line up with words in other languages easily" ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/letter-letter-word-word-interview-david-j-peterson/)). Design those as root families early, before contributors each map them to English on their own.

Vocabulary should be densest where the culture's stakes are. Frommer: "If something is important to a culture, there will be an efficient way of talking about that thing in the language" ([Nautilus](https://lede-v2.nautil.us/14-words-for-horse-the-linguistics-of-game-of-thrones-234392)). Dothraki has 14 words for horse and none for toilet (same source). Klingon's closest thing to "hello," *nuqneH*, means "What do you want?" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_language)). Old Norse supplies a culture-specific number system, the **long hundred of 120**: *tvau hundrað* is 240 and *þúsund* is 1,200, with a separate "decimal hundred" term once outsiders' counting arrived ([Wikipedia: Long hundred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_hundred)). That gives Norsund a native count plus a "trade hundred" for Kedre merchants. Kinship offers six classic systems, from the Hawaiian type, which distinguishes only sex and generation, to the Sudanese type, which names every cousin separately ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinship_terminology)). For DDU the dense fields are monster-hunting and guild ranks (the Lexipedia includes reference lists of guilds), canals and watercraft, crystal caverns, oaths, and the sky. A single star root can carry fate, guidance, holiness, navigation and luck. Poetic coinage gets its own grammar through **kennings and heiti**. A kenning is a base word plus a determinant, as in *báru fákr* "wave's horse" for ship. Kennings can nest (*tvíkent*); Snorri accepted up to five elements and condemned mixed metaphor (*nykrat*). Heiti are single-word poetic synonyms, like *fákr* beside prose *hestr* "horse" ([Wikipedia: Kenning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenning)). A rule of at most two levels in speech and five in formal verse lets bards coin vocabulary that can be checked against a rule.

Naming systems come with documented templates and tests. A Viking-Age name was usually a given name from a thin stock, a patronymic (extendable by the grandfather's name), and a byname that often did the real identifying. Bynames could be unflattering, heroic, physical or about dress, and could replace the given name entirely ([Ruiter, Aberdeen](https://www.abdn.ac.uk/news/8745/)). Norse toponyms pair a specific with a generic from a small set: *-by* "farmstead," *-thorpe* "offshoot settlement," *-holm* "marsh island," *-thwaite* "clearing," *-wick* "bay," *-ness* "headland." Hybrids such as *Grimston* (Norse personal name plus English *-ton*) mark contact ([viking.no](https://www.viking.no/the-viking-world/the-vikings-and-the-law/danelaw/place-names-in-the-danelaw-2/)). Norsund needs its own generics for canals, star shrines, caverns and lairs. A naming guide written for tabletop players reports that its author's names averaged **8.2 letters against 5.6 for common US names**. It recommends distinct initial sounds for major characters and a read-aloud "multiplication table" of morpheme combinations to catch comic results. The author dropped *-bal* that way ([Troy Press](https://troypress.com/a-naming-language-for-use-by-players)). Peterson's consistency rule is the famous one: no lone *Skrux* among *Manta, Kalu, Sevan* ([Peterson](https://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/fl-00005A-00.pdf)). His essay title, "Names Aren't Neutral," carries a warning that bears directly on Kedre. Faux-Latin endings (*-us, -ia, -os, -ion*) read as noble to English audiences, while non-European-sounding naming has historically been handed to villains. A Latinate tyrant-empire therefore plays against audience priors, which is useful. The reverse trap is giving harsh, exotic sound profiles to "monstrous" peoples such as the Kullmyr purely to signal villainy.

## A short imperial occupation should leave words, not grammar

Every expert describes the same diachronic workflow. Sketch a proto-language. Apply an ordered list of regular sound changes that target categories of sounds (all voiced stops between vowels, say) rather than single phonemes. Layer grammatical change and borrowing on top. Repeat for each daughter language or dialect ([Ask Zompist](https://zompist.wordpress.com/2019/01/26/ask-zompist-common-sound-changes/)). Sound change "has no memory." When it makes two words collide, four outcomes are possible: homophony, merged meaning, one word resisting, or one word being replaced ([Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Conlang/Intermediate/History/Common_sound_changes)). Rosenfelder's stopping rule is to keep adding changes until every word in your sample has been altered, calibrated against real depths: **Latin to French is about 2,000 years; Old to Modern German about 1,000** ([Ask Zompist](https://zompist.wordpress.com/2019/01/26/ask-zompist-common-sound-changes/)). David Peterson's distinctive move was to build a sound profile of Ghiscari from the names in the books and let it choose which changes turned High Valyrian into Astapori and Meereenese ([Little White Lies, snippet](https://lwlies.com/articles/how-do-you-create-a-real-fake-language/)). Tolkien's Sindarin is documented the same way, as ordered, stage-tagged rules such as the "i-fronting" stage of i-affection ([Eldamo](https://eldamo.org/content/words/word-3958031275.html)).

Calibrated to DDU's timeline, that implies moderate change. The records place the first settlers' arrival from the island of Farheim at 450 b.v.e., the founding of Marheim at 432 b.v.e., the imperial invasion at 37 a.v.e., the rune-cataclysm at 50, and Auristar's walls rising past 124 a.v.e. That is about 575 documented years, a little over half of Rosenfelder's Old-to-Modern-German benchmark. Old Norsund (already attested in lore as the source of *Vilkyr*) should be hard but partly readable to modern speakers. A deeper Proto-Norsund can sit in Farheim before the settlement.

| Stage | Anchor in the DDU timeline | What happens | Real-world model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proto-Norsund | Farheim, before 450 b.v.e. | Roots, ablaut grades, umlaut-triggering suffixes | Proto-Germanic; Tolkien's Common Eldarin |
| Old Norsund | Settlement through the Marheim era | i-umlaut, breaking, nominative *-r*; saga, law and oath register | Old Norse; Old Sindarin |
| Contact Norsund | Occupation, 37 a.v.e., and its aftermath | Kedre loans in layers; little grammatical change | English after 1066 |
| Modern Norsund | Present | Innovative Auristar dialect; conservative Vilnayan and Farheim speech | Venetian versus rural varieties; Peterson's Dragonstone |

The occupation's length decides what Kedre leaves behind. Peterson's working numbers are that a language takes about **three generations** to vanish from a family, and five or six for rulers to stamp it out deliberately. In his Ghiscari Valyrian, the conquerors' grammar won and the conquered language survived as vocabulary ([dothraki.com](https://dothraki.com/2014/05/the-state-of-valyrian/)). DDU's lore gives Kedre a 13-year siege and a few decades of presence, unless earlier trade contact is added. That is too short for language shift and points to **lexical borrowing in prestige domains rather than restructuring**. The model is a superstrate that the Germanic language survives, as in English after the Norman Conquest. Normandy is the mirror case: there Romance won, yet Norse left about 150 words, many of them maritime (*hommet* "islet" < *hólmr*, *nez* "headland" < *nes*) ([Wikipedia: Norman language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_language)). Real contact layers cluster by domain. Sicilian's roughly 300 Arabic words are mostly agriculture and irrigation ([Wikipedia: Sicilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_language)). Kedre loans should cluster the same way, in imperial administration, law, the Church of Light, alchemy (the lore's Imperial Academy of Alchemy), siegecraft and luxury goods. Sea, weather, kinship and the body stay native. The lore already models resistance in the sacred domain: the goddess Kedre calls *Luxis* is *Lysmar* in Norsund, a native name rather than a loan. *Lysmar* and *Luxis* also look like cognates, which forces a foundational decision. Are Kedre and Norsund related at depth, as Latin and the Germanic languages are? Shared ancestry would supply "free" cognate pairs, and it has to be settled before Proto-Norsund roots are fixed.

Kedre loans should enter in three strata that produce doublets. Early popular loans undergo every later Norsund sound change and end up looking native; *mur* in *Kjoldmur* is a candidate. Occupation-era administrative loans are only partly adapted, with Kedre's final vowels clipped to fit Norsund's closed syllables. Late learned loans, taken from written Kedre by scholars, stay nearly pristine. The same Kedre root borrowed twice yields a worn-down and a pristine form that can drift apart in meaning, the way Sicilian has words for "twins" from five layers at once: *giameddi*, *bizzuni*, *binelli*, *èmmuli*, *ièmiddi* ([Wikipedia: Sicilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_language)). Norman toponymy shows the adaptation rules in action, in reverse: Norse *Ketill* became *Quetteville* and *Sigfríðr* became *Chiffre-* ([Wikipedia: Norman toponymy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_toponymy)). One dependency follows from Peterson's method. Kedre's sound profile should steer both loan adaptation and Auristar's dialect changes, so **Kedre's phonology must be designed before Norsund's contact stage**.

Dialects are the same proto-forms run through partly different rule sets, and Peterson's Valyrian sets the social logic. Meereen diverged most because it is the largest city with the biggest lower class. Isolated Dragonstone stayed conservative. Yunkish was left nearly identical to Astapori to save effort ([dothraki.com](https://dothraki.com/2014/05/the-state-of-valyrian/)). Rosenfelder's research found that the ongoing sound changes he identified were "divergences from the standard, rather than imitations of society's leaders" ([Zompist blog, snippet](https://zompist.wordpress.com/2012/06/)). Auristar is the largest, richest city in Thiolden (the records give 523,208 people), so it should be phonologically *innovative* even if its written standard is archaizing. Venetian supplies the changes, applied to Norsund itself rather than as Italian vocabulary. Venetian has no geminates (Italian *fette, palla, penna* are Venetian *féte, bała, péna*). Its "evanescent l" lets *góndoła* become *góndoa* and merges *scóła* "school" with *scóa* "broom." It drops final vowels after continuants, velarizes final *-n*, stigmatizes [θ] and replaces it with [s] or [ts], and writes /z/ with ⟨x⟩ (*xe*, medieval *caxa*) ([Wikipedia: Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_language)). A city that degeminates, loses intervocalic *l*, and says [s] where villagers keep [θ] gets a "Viking Venice" sound with no Italian words at all. *Thiolden* itself, with its initial *th*, becomes a shibboleth. The insular-versus-mainland split also has a ready template in West versus East Norse: diphthongs kept versus merged, more versus less u-umlaut, and an epenthetic vowel whose color varied by region (*aftur/aftir/aftær*) ([Wikipedia: Old Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse)). That fits Farheim and the Vilnayan towns.

Registers complete the picture. In Ferguson's diglossia, a codified high variety carries writing, liturgy and law over a vernacular low variety. Fishman extended the concept to unrelated languages, as with French over Alsatian ([Wikipedia: Diglossia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diglossia)). Old Norsund can serve as the native high register for oaths, saga and law. It would use heiti and kennings and keep distinctions the vernacular lost, the way Norse poetry kept *fákr* beside prose *hestr* ([Wikipedia: Kenning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenning)). Kedre can be the foreign high register of scholarship, trade and imperial law. The obvious default would make Kedre the liturgical language of Auristar's star cult, but the lore pulls the other way. The city was founded by survivors of an imperial rune-cataclysm and worships new gods revealed after the emperor's death. This report therefore recommends a **native sacred register** and keeping Kedre for learning; the community should settle that point explicitly. Star vocabulary can then exist in three tiers (a native kenning, an Old Norsund heiti, a learned Kedre loan), chosen by register. No sourced guidance on conlang honorific design turned up, so a Kedre-derived formal layer in the city against kin-term address in the countryside remains a plausible but unverified pattern.

Tooling makes all of this reproducible. Rosenfelder's SCA² takes sound categories, ordered `X/Y/environment` rules and a lexicon, and can display intermediate stages ([Zompist: SCA²](https://www.zompist.com/sca2.html)). Lexurgy is a GPL-3.0 Kotlin applier with a command-line tool and a web app ([GitHub](https://github.com/def-gthill/lexurgy)). The Index Diachronica (version 10.2) catalogs attested sound changes, searchable by feature ([chridd](https://chridd.nfshost.com/diachronica/)). Sound-change appliers "struggle with syllable-specific changes like syncope and multi-syllable environments like umlaut" ([Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Conlang/Intermediate/History/Common_sound_changes)). For a Norse-like language, that means implementing umlaut in two steps: mark the vowel when *i/j* follows, then delete the trigger. Kedre loans should be injected as a separate input list at the contact stage so that they only undergo later rules. That mechanically produces Peterson's "masterful" stratification. With the rule files version-controlled next to the lexicon, any rule change regenerates every word consistently.

## Auristar's star script should be engineered as the anti-rune

Script type should follow the language. "Syllabaries work for languages with simple syllable structures"; abjads suit consonant-root morphology ([UCL PLIN0068](https://www.homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~ucjtudo/assets/teaching/PLIN0068/4_writing_systems.html)). "An alphabet is still the easiest type of script to make a font for," while abugidas and syllabaries need many ligatures ([Conlang Guide](https://conlang-guide.bearblog.dev/deep-dives-writing-systems/)). Norsund's consonant clusters and umlaut vowel contrasts rule out a syllabary or an abjad. A featural layer can sit on top of any type: in Tengwar, four series by place of articulation and six grades by manner make doubling a bow voice a stop and raising a stem make a fricative ([Wikipedia: Tengwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengwar)). That is the strongest way to make a script read as engineered rather than a cipher. And "a writing system is not a language," so Norsund can legitimately have several scripts (same Conlang Guide source).

Real runic history supplies worldbuilding beats almost ready-made. The Elder Futhark had 24 runes in three groups of eight (*ættir*), used from the 2nd to the 8th century, with about 350 known inscriptions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elder_Futhark)). By the late 8th century it had shrunk to the **16-rune Younger Futhark**, even as sound changes were *adding* phonemes. One rune came to cover u, v, w, y, o and ø; voicing went unwritten; and a habit of never carving the same rune twice in a row erased vowel length, so different words were spelled identically ([Wikipedia: Younger Futhark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Younger_Futhark)). That ambiguity hands DDU an in-world reason rune magic is dangerous. In *Egil's Saga*, a suitor's "ten spell-words writ wrongly" on a whalebone sicken a girl, and Egil warns that no one should carve runes who cannot read them ([National Museum of Denmark](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/religion-magic-death-and-rituals/runic-magic/)). DDU's lore already frames rune danger as a matter of placement errors. Scholarly honesty cuts against the romance, though. The National Museum of Denmark judges that "runic magic seems more of a Medieval and Early Modern invention than a Viking-Age fact," and most inscriptions are mundane (same source). The popular theory that runes avoid horizontal strokes because of wood grain is an assumption: early inscriptions do have horizontals ([Wikipedia: Runes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runes)). DDU's literal rune magic is legitimate fantasy license. It is best kept alongside ordinary rune use, which one user story already voices: "the runes are just the written word of the people alive at that time."

History also models how a forbidden script survives. Iceland held 120 witch trials between 1604 and 1720, and a Danish-imposed law banned owning magic books. No source confirms a ban on runes *as letters* ([Wikipedia: Witch trials in Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witch_trials_in_Iceland)). In England, runes gave way to a church-backed Latin alphabet, which absorbed the runic letters þorn and wynn ([Wikipedia: Anglo-Saxon runes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_runes)). After Tolkien's Tengwar spread, his Cirth were "mostly limited to carved inscriptions" ([Wikipedia: Cirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirth)). Auristar should therefore ban rune *casting*, not rune *reading*. That keeps archivists, contraband slates and old monuments in play, and a few "defanged" rune-derived letters absorbed into the star script give in-world scholars something to argue about.

Medium shapes letters, and DDU's visual brief names the medium. Wedge-tipped styluses pressed into clay produced cuneiform's five basic wedges ([Wikipedia: Cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform)). Palm leaves are thought to have produced the curls of Brahmic scripts ([UCL](https://www.homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~ucjtudo/assets/teaching/PLIN0068/4_writing_systems.html)). Ogham was cut along the edge of a standing stone, which served as its stemline ([Wikipedia: Ogham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogham)). Auristar is carved white stone and stained-glass domes, and stained glass is lead lines enclosing colored cells. Glyphs built from **closed cells and continuous lines**, using the curves, horizontals, enclosed spaces and radial symmetry that runes avoid, would read as the anti-rune at a glance and translate directly into glass and inlay. The scripts that succeed on screen each have one physical metaphor. Dovahzul was meant to look "carved into stone with a dragon's talon" ([Bethesda](https://elderscrolls.bethesda.net/en-AU/news/how-bethesda-game-studios-made-skyrims-dragon-language)). Klingon's pIqaD drew on bladed weapons and hull markings ([Wikipedia: Klingon scripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_scripts)). Aurebesh was standardized on Eurostile-like shapes ([Wikipedia: Aurebesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurebesh)). Neography's process advice converges on a few steps. Pick a small set of reusable strokes, simplify by writing fast to get a cursive, test at small sizes, and avoid glyphs that are mirrors or rotations of one another, since letters like b/p/d/q "make reading harder for people with dyslexia" ([Neography.info](https://neography.info/create-a-script/)). In practice that means five to seven primitives (dot, ray, arc, ring, Auristar's seven-point star, a horizon line), with ray count or a halo encoding sound features in Tengwar style. The fine rays will blur first at UI sizes.

Players will decode the script whatever DDU does, so make decoding teach Norsund. Aurebesh, Daedric and Twilight Princess Hylian are Latin-letter ciphers. Tunic's designer avoided plain transliteration precisely because players test for ciphers ([Wikipedia: Tunic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunic_(video_game))), yet players cracked it as phonetic English within about a month ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/a-tunic-player-has-translated-the-in-game-language-3197321)). A star script mapped to *Norsund* phonemes rather than English letters rewards decoders with the language itself, and lore can be hidden in it. Even a principled script has limits, though. The KLI's one-to-one pIqaD exists, yet "the vast majority of Klingonists prefer Latin-based romanization" ([Wikipedia: Klingon scripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_scripts)). Lucasfilm's condition that Aurebesh be presented as one of many alphabets is a useful precedent for leaving room for regional variants. One real-world hazard needs a formal gate. A clothing logo combining two runes caused controversy for resembling SS insignia ([Wikipedia: Bind rune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bind_rune)), so every rune, bind rune and logo should be screened against hate-symbol databases.

Font engineering favors a hybrid. Unity's TextMesh Pro supports only the kern, liga, mark and mkmk OpenType features, and it does not read GPOS-stored kerning ([Unity OTL](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.textmeshpro@4.0/api/UnityEngine.TextCore.OTL_FeatureTag.html); [Unity TMP](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.textmeshpro@4.0/manual/FontAssetsCreator.html)). The game-UI font should therefore be a cipher font over the Latin romanization, with simple ligatures and no contextual shaping. For fans who want to type the script, Private Use Area code points have "no standard meaning" ([Wikipedia: PUA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_Use_Areas)), so registering a block with the Under-ConScript Unicode Registry avoids collisions ([Kreative Korp](https://www.kreativekorp.com/ucsur/)). Toki Pona's sitelen pona, added there in August 2021, ships fonts that turn typed ASCII words into glyphs through ligatures ([sona.pona.la](https://sona.pona.la/wiki/sitelen_pona_font_guidelines)). Real runes already occupy U+16A0–U+16FF ([Wikipedia: Elder Futhark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elder_Futhark)), so DDU runes with reassigned values should not be fonted over that block. Carved stone and glass need no font at all: draw them from a master glyph sheet that shares stroke primitives with the UI font.

Romanization should be boring on purpose. David Peterson: it "should be as uncreative as humanly possible… There should be no need for a page saying how each letter is pronounced." He adds: "If most readers will be American, avoid diacritics at all costs, as these will be ignored." Apostrophes are legitimate for possession, contraction, glottal stops or ejectives; otherwise "using an apostrophe to separate parts of a word is not clever: it is an abomination" ([Peterson](https://fiatlingua.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/fl-00005A-00.pdf)). Rosenfelder: "never use a diacritical mark without giving it a specific meaning," and "please don't browse the Unicode catalog as if all the characters were just decoration." Even Tolkien's hard *c* is still mispronounced ([Rosenfelder, LCK](https://www.zompist.com/kitlong.html)). Two defensible conventions compete for Norsund's canonical form: normalized-Old-Norse letters (þ, ð, æ, ø, acute accents), which Norse-literate fans expect, or plain ASCII. The records settle it: *Kjoldmur*, *Starifjolden* and *Thiolden* are already ASCII. **Make ASCII canonical**, with *k* rather than *c*, *j* always /j/ (unproblematic after consonants, as in *kj* and *fj*, and covered in the pronunciation guide), and *th* for /θ/. Reserve þ, ð and ø for citing Old Norsund, the way scholars normalize Old Norse. Even scholarly Old Norse spelling bent to technology: *ö* displaced *ǫ* because early character sets lacked it ([Wikipedia: Old Norse orthography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse_orthography)). The orthography then becomes a historical layer: *Thiolden* in modern spelling, *Þiolden* in the sagas. Rosenfelder suggests a distinct romanization style per nation ([Rosenfelder, LCK](https://www.zompist.com/kitlong.html)). That fits Kedre's existing Latinate look (*Luxis*, *Keras*, *Rechtar*) and an Auristar civic spelling tradition with a Venetian-style ⟨x⟩ for /z/. The apostrophes in *Thor'Vale*, *Karhak'Modan* and *Ik'nal'tar* each need one stated value in their own language (a glottal stop is fine for a non-Norsund tongue) or should be removed. Klingon carries a font lesson too: its capital *I* is confusable with *l* in sans-serif type ([Wikipedia: Klingon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_language)). Choose a UI typeface that clearly separates þ from p and ð from d.

## Studios ship grammar, a scoped lexicon and audio, then absorb drift

Professional scope is "grammar plus enough words for the script plus margin." In 2009 HBO had the Language Creation Society run a contest. Applicants devised a phonology and lexicon and translated pilot dialogue, and Peterson beat about 30 to 35 proposals ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_J._Peterson)). By November he had a "more or less complete grammar" and about 1,700 words ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/meet-game-of-thrones-resident-linguist-ki-fin-yeni-1798269356)). Lexicons then grow three- to six-fold over a decade as sequels and fans create demand. *House of the Dragon* needed more High Valyrian than Peterson expected ([Wikipedia: Valyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valyrian_languages)). The delivery format is standard. Producers mark lines, and the conlanger returns translations plus audio at normal speed, slow speed and syllable by syllable ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/baltimore/news/the-game-of-thrones-language-that-1-2m-people-are-learning)). One actor described the packet as "the English translation, the High Valyrian, a phonetic translation, and then also audio recordings" ([Drama Quarterly](https://dramaquarterly.com/talking-my-language/)). Okrand worked from script excerpts alone, delivered transcripts and MP3s, and visited the set once ([StarTrek.com](https://www.startrek.com/news/qapla-klingon-language-creator-marc-okrand-part-2)).

The best single artifact to copy is Peterson's four-page *High Valyrian Pronunciation Guide* (November 2020). Every letter gets an anchor word plus a "never like…" warning ("like the 'g' in 'goat' (never like the 'g' in 'genius')"). Unaspirated stops get a hand-in-front-of-your-face test, and one compact stress rule covers the rest. It also states where variation is allowed: *r* varies "depending on the original language of the speaker" ([dedalvs.com](https://dedalvs.com/work/house-of-the-dragon/misc/pronunciation_guide.pdf)). That turns accents into lore rather than error, which matters for a setting "inhabited by many conscious fantasy races."

Drift is guaranteed, and the industry absorbs it instead of fighting it. Peterson is not on set, so "mispronunciations have been inevitable." Showrunners overrode his pronunciation of *khaleesi*, and editors clip sentences ([TED Ideas](https://ideas.ted.com/?p=111825)). TNG staff wrote most later Klingon themselves, and Okrand ruled that "any Klingon spoken during TNG counts as legitimate Klingon, whether I made it up or not." His own mistakes, he said, become "part of the language just because I said it" ([StarTrek.com](https://www.startrek.com/news/qapla-klingon-language-creator-marc-okrand-part-2)). Skyrim is the cautionary case for parallel creators. Dovahzul was "first and foremost, a gameplay device for using the shouts," built on "smoke and mirrors." Its English-Dragon database fell behind because "once we got rolling, the designers started coming up with some new words and phrases." Having no generative depth, it lacked a word for "love" until the designer coined *Ros* for a fan's wedding cake years later ([Bethesda](https://elderscrolls.bethesda.net/en-AU/news/how-bethesda-game-studios-made-skyrims-dragon-language)). Filling gaps without a generative system and provenance draws a lasting charge of illegitimacy: purists call some film-era Neo-Sindarin "not Sindarin but an arbitrary fabrication" ([Tolkien Gateway](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Neo-Elvish)).

DDU's decentralized creators are TNG's staff writers and Skyrim's designers at scale, so Norsund needs a **writer-and-actor kit at version 1.0**. It should contain a one-page pronunciation guide in the High Valyrian format, a naming guide with templates and banned clusters, and 50 to 100 free-use flavor words and set phrases (greetings, oaths, curses, toasts, star terms). Add reference audio for names, and a rule that any full Norsund sentence in a canon product passes through the language process and gets logged with a source tag. Wenja shows the upside of investment: Ubisoft's *Far Cry Primal* cast, trained by immersion, soon "started randomly shouting out things in Wenja" ([Wikipedia: Wenja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenja_language)). The research found no primary statement from Peterson, Okrand or Frommer on when to subtitle. The working heuristic is to subtitle when the plot depends on the content, leave lines opaque when they are texture, and repeat set phrases until audiences learn them.

## Canon is a governance status, because nobody owns a language

Every durable conlang community separates a protected core from a growing periphery, attaches a citation to every word, and names a decider. The models differ mainly in who decides.

| Model | Exemplar | Protected core | Growth path | Decider | Known weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Creator plus archivist | Klingon / KLI | Okrand's grammar | "Only words from confirmed Okrand sources… and the source must be cited" ([KLI](https://www.kli.org/about-klingon/new-klingon-words/)) | Okrand; a KLI team curates | Founder dependency; "unclear how much neologism is acceptable" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon_language)) |
| Gatekeeper plus pipeline | Na'vi / LEP | Frommer's grammar | Anonymous, rule-checked proposals released in batches ([naviteri.org](https://naviteri.org/)) | "I am still the only gatekeeper" ([Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-features/avatar-the-way-of-water-real-life-navi-language-discord-james-cameron-depression-1234649601)) | Single point of failure |
| Founding text plus academy | Esperanto | *Fundamento*, which "no one has the right to change" (1905) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamento_de_Esperanto)) | Official Additions of equal authority | Akademio, 45 members on nine-year terms ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akademio_de_Esperanto)) | Slow |
| Frozen baseline | Lojban | Baseline frozen for at least five years | Only compounds, borrowings and names stay open ([LLG](https://tiki.lojban.org/Official+Baseline+Statement)) | Commission biased toward the status quo | Its own chair argued in 2010 that the policy caused serious harm ([Lojban list](https://groups.google.com/g/lojban/c/M9Jug4X-0pc)) |
| Usage survey plus dataset | Toki Pona / Linku | Sonja Lang's books | Annual survey tiers from "core" (90–100% use) down to "sandbox" ([sona.pona.la](https://sona.pona.la/wiki/usage_surveys)) | Respondents (345 in 2022) | Lags design intent; needs a speaker base |

Three facts frame DDU's choice. First, **no case turned up of a conlang whose canon is decided by direct contributor or token vote**. The nearest analogues are an elected academy and a usage survey, and the research found no evidence on how vote-based canon handles brigaded proposals. Second, single-gatekeeper models depend on aging founders with no published succession rules, while institutions (the Akademio) and processes (Linku) have outlived theirs. Third, Linku proves that the decentralized mechanics work. Its dataset keeps one TOML file per word on GitHub, generates schemas and type-checks every entry in a validation pipeline, accepts changes by pull request, and is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 ([GitHub: lipu-linku/sona](https://github.com/lipu-linku/sona)). The synthesis for Norsund is a **"Norsund Fundamento" v1.0.0** covering phonology, phonotactics, romanization, core morphology and roughly 100 to 200 function and core words. Changing it should take a major version, a supermajority and a time lock, plus a *scheduled* review path so the Lojban stall cannot recur. Lexical additions would be minor versions and corrections patches, and derived products would declare "Norsund v1.x compliant."

Automation should shrink what voters argue about. CI should check phonotactics, stress, homophone collisions, forbidden clusters and schema validity before any vote, as Linku validates entries and as LEP proposals must already follow Frommer's word-building and syllable rules ([Kelutral](https://kelutral.org/references/wordbuilding)). Votes then decide meaning and taste only. A small elected steward role should hold a narrow, published veto limited to grammatical and phonological non-compliance. Anonymous submission, the LEP's practice, reduces social pressure where contributors hold votes. A sandbox tier lets products use unratified words if labeled. Published coinage rules (approved affixes, derivation patterns, kenning grammar, naming templates) make rule-following coinages canon-by-rule before formal ratification, which closes the neologism gap that dogs Klingon. Deviations that ship in a product become recorded variants if they pass core checks, following Okrand. DDU's existing `fact-record/v1` format already carries `contributor` and `needsReview` fields, which map naturally onto provenance and the sandbox tier. A lexeme record in that spirit, using only what the lore attests, would look like this:

```yaml
lemma: vilkyr
plural: vilkyren
glosses: [free sword, monster-hunter]       # at least two, per LCK anti-relex rule
morphemes: [vil "free", kyr "sword"]       # proposed analysis
epoch: old-norsund                          # entry stage, for stratification
stratum: native                             # native | kedre-a | kedre-b | kedre-c
register: [common, old-norsund]
status: attested-legacy                     # sandbox | proposed | canon | variant | deprecated
source: records/user-submitted-stories/dvwl.yaml
ratified_in: null                           # e.g. norsund-v1.0.0 vote id
ipa: TBD
```

The legal ground is clearer than it looks, though unsettled. No court has ruled that a constructed language is or is not copyrightable. In *Paramount v. Axanar*, the Language Creation Society's amicus brief (April 27, 2016) argued that "a constructed language *itself* is not protected, and should not be. Copyright law is simply too blunt a tool for this." The court declined on May 10, 2016 to "reach the issue of whether languages, and specifically the Klingon language, are copyrightable." Its January 2017 order said only that Klingon "may not be individually original and copyright protectable," and a pretrial ruling barred Paramount from telling the jury it owns Klingon ([LCS](https://conlang.org/axanar/)). Pamela Samuelson locates the doctrine in §102(b)'s exclusion of "systems" and "methods of operation," which codifies *Baker v. Selden* ([Berkeley Law](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/center-article/why-copyright-law-excludes-systems-and-processes-from-the-scope-of-its-protection/)). Peterson's version: "you can copyright the book but not the words in it or how people use them" ([Salon](https://www.salon.com/2016/05/04/does_klingon_belong_to_everyone_its_a_language_the_whole_point_is_to_use_it)). The steelman on the other side, from at least one commentator, holds that a language created for a franchise gives the studio a "strong case" ([IP Intelligence Report](https://www.ipintelligencereport.com/blogs/is-paramount-going-where-no-copyright-holder-has-gone-before)). In practice Paramount controls officialness through the books, the trademarks and its June 2016 fan-film guidelines, which the Axanar settlement of January 20, 2017 bound the defendants to follow. A 2023 arbitration later awarded Paramount and CBS $292,372.54 in costs for breach ([Space.com](https://www.space.com/35436-star-trek-axanar-fan-film-lawsuit-settled.html); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2023/10/23/axanar-creator-faces-new-legal-threat/amp/)). Communities honor the line voluntarily: the KLI's new-words list omits dictionary vocabulary "in order not to compete with Dr. Okrand's income via royalties" ([KLI](https://www.kli.org/about-klingon/new-klingon-words/)).

For a franchise without a corporate owner, that argues for **CC0 or CC BY on the lexicon and grammar data**, which maximizes embedding by games and merchandise. Linku's share-alike license keeps derivatives open but can deter studios. Contributions should come in under a DCO sign-off or CLA so individual coinages never become ownership disputes. "Official Norsund" should be a badge, granted by the validator against a declared version and backed by DDU or Norsund marks and permissive derived-product guidelines. Three questions remain open and need legal review: whether the EU's database right attaches to a curated lexicon, how contributor moral rights interact with CC0, and how non-US law treats languages at all.

Adoption follows exposure plus an on-ramp plus ritual. Duolingo's High Valyrian course, written and voiced by Peterson, launched in July 2017 with more than 2,000 words ([Quartz](https://qz.com/1027693/game-of-thrones-duolingo-now-offers-a-high-valyrian-language-course)) and had about 740,000 active learners by 2019 ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/2019/03/13/how-one-linguist-teaching-game-thrones-fans-how-speak-high-valyrian)). Klingon's course launched in March 2018 after more than 170,000 people asked for it ([TribLive](https://archive.triblive.com/business/technology/duolingo-launches-klingon-language-learning-course)). The long-lived communities run on calendars: Okrand's word reveals at the KLI's annual qep'a', Frommer's Na'viteri batches (still posting in October 2026), and Linku's survey on Toki Pona's anniversary, whose dataset feeds several downstream sites and a Discord bot ([linku.la](https://linku.la/about)). Periodic, changelogged "Norsund lexicon releases" give DDU both the ritual and clean version targets. A stable YAML schema lets one dataset feed games, bots, flashcards and fonts at once.

## Conclusion

The textbook order (sounds, words, grammar, script) is the wrong order for DDU's *first* move. Every franchise language that worked began by analyzing a small corpus that already existed, and DDU's corpus is already in its records, with its morpheme regularities, its drift and its collisions. The highest-leverage step is therefore not designing a phoneme inventory in the abstract. It is freezing and segmenting the attested names, deciding whether Kedre and Norsund share an ancestor, and publishing a phonotactic validator, all before any grammar is written. The diachronic model and the governance pipeline also turn out to be the same data structure. The fields that let an expert achieve Peterson's "masterful" stratification (entry epoch, loan stratum, derivation) sit right next to the fields a decentralized canon needs (provenance, status, version). A git-native lore repository makes the hardest naturalism goal in conlanging nearly free, provided every lexeme record carries both kinds of field from day one.

The likeliest way Norsund fails is not bad linguistics. It is parallel creators coining faster than any steward can review, which already outran Skyrim's database and is visible in miniature in DDU's own spelling variants. Votes are the wrong tool for phonology and the right tool for meaning, so DDU's distinctive bet should be to automate everything rule-checkable and let the community vote only on what rules cannot decide. That bet is genuinely untested: no known conlang settles canon this way. Norsund could become the precedent, provided the core is frozen before the first vote.
