# Learn Norsund in 10 lessons

A short course for fans, writers and players. Each lesson takes about 15 minutes. It uses the real texts of Thiolden, so by the end you'll read the gate inscription of the Kjoldmur, the Vilkyr oath and the Prophecy of the Auristar. The answers are at the bottom.

You'll need [pronunciation-guide.md](pronunciation-guide.md) open for lesson 1, and you'll find every word in the [dictionary](dictionary.md). To check your own sentences, run `node tools/cli.mjs gloss "<sentence>"`, or use the [Norsund Workbench](../../../tools/web/).

## Lesson 1: Sounds and greetings

**The one rule:** stress the first syllable of every word. THYOL-den, OW-ree-star, VIL-kü-ren.

**Four sounds English speakers get wrong:**

| Spelling | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *j* | always "y" | *Starifjolden* STAR-ee-fyol-den |
| *y* | "ee" with rounded lips | *kyr* KÜR |
| *kj* | "ky", one sound | *Kjoldmur* KYOLD-moor |
| *th* | always as in "thin" or "this" | *thiol* THYOL |

**Words**

| Norsund | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Lys dy!* | Hello! (lit. light to you) |
| *Eth dy lys!* | And light to you! (the reply) |
| *Thiol!* | Hail! (hunters' greeting; lit. brave!) |
| *Velk!* | Welcome! |
| *Mund!* | Thanks! |
| *Nekut.* | You're welcome. (lit. nothing) |
| *Starkjold!* | Farewell! |
| *Kjal!* | Cheers! |

**Exercises**

1.1 Say these aloud, stressing the first syllable: *Thiolden, Auristar, Vilkyren, Starirauggheim, Midelmar.*

1.2 Someone says *Lys dy!* What do you answer?

1.3 You're given a drink. What do you say? And when you leave the tavern?

## Lesson 2: Things and people: "the" and plurals

Every noun is **animate** (people, beasts, gods) or **inanimate** (everything else).

| | Animate (*Vilkyr*, a free sword) | Inanimate (*kyr*, a blade) |
|---|---|---|
| a / one | Vilkyr | kyr |
| several | Vilkyr**en** | kyr**ar** |
| the | Vilkyr**in** | kyr**in** |
| the (several) | Vilkyr**ene** | kyr**arne** |

There is no word for "a": *kyr* means "a blade". "The" is an ending: *kyrin* "the blade".

**Words:** *ulf* wolf (an.), *hest* horse (an.), *star* star (in.), *mur* wall (in.), *heim* home (in.), *gat* goat (an.; plural *get*).

**Exercises**

2.1 Give "the", "several" and "the several" forms of *ulf* and *mur*.

2.2 Translate: *hestin, starar, Vilkyrene, heimarne.*

2.3 *Gat* is one of six nouns that change their vowel in the plural. What are "goats" and "the goats"?

## Lesson 3: Being and describing

*Vesa* "to be": present **es**, past **vas**, for every person. Adjectives never change and go before the noun: *aur star* "a silver star", *aur starin* "the silver star".

**Words:** *thiol* brave, *skarp* sharp, *kvit* white, *dalg* warm, *kjeldug* cold, *myd* tired, *glyppug* hungry, *thrakug* strong.

**Examples** (from Kilia's song)

```
Fyr-in es dalg, aul-in es kjeldug.
fire-DEF be.PRS warm ale-DEF be.PRS cold
"The fire is warm, the ale is cool."
```

On mottos and carvings, *es* is dropped: *Vil kyrin, vothen sundin.* "Free (is) the blade, bound (is) the word."

**Exercises**

3.1 Translate into Norsund: *The blades are sharp. The horse is strong. The star is silver-bright.*

3.2 Translate: *Aulin vas dalg.*

3.3 Make *thiol* into a noun: "the brave one", "the brave (people)".

## Lesson 4: I, you, she, it

| | Subject | Object | My/your/… |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | em | om | emi |
| you | dy | du | dys |
| he/she/they (one being) | sel | sal | seli |
| it | at | at | ati |
| we | emen | omen | emeni |
| you (several) | dyn | dun | dyni |
| they (beings) | selen | salen | seleni |

*Sel* is for any person or creature, whatever their sex. Possessives replace "the": *emi brod* "my sibling".

**Exercises**

4.1 Translate: *Em es myd. Dy es thiol. Sel vas glyppug.*

4.2 Translate into Norsund: *My sibling is a Vilkyr. Her horse is white. Your (several) home is beautiful (fjelg).*

4.3 Fill in the object form: *Em sajer ___* (I see him). *Sel sajer ___* (She sees me).

## Lesson 5: Doing: present and past

Every verb's dictionary form ends in **-a**. Take it off, then add:

| Present | Past | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -er | -ade | *kjolda* "guard": *kjolder* "guards", *kjoldade* "guarded" |

Verbs don't change for person: *em kjolder, dy kjolder, selen kjolder*.

About twenty old verbs change their vowel in the past instead:

| Verb | Meaning | Past |
|---|---|---|
| *dola* | fall | **dul** |
| *lida* | come | **leid** |
| *gana* | go | **gon** |
| *bita* | bite | **beit** |
| *saja* | see | **sa** |

**Words:** *kjala* drink, *thiarka* hunt, *nura* heal, *sunda* sing, *skrimm* monster.

**Exercises**

5.1 Translate: *Astrid kjaladade aul.* Careful: one of these forms is wrong. Which, and what should it be?

5.2 Translate into Norsund:
- Tyr hunted the monster.
- Haakon healed the people (*lydene*).
- The wolf bit the goat.

5.3 Translate: *Em gon ve kjalheimin. Vadanna leid i nimin.*

## Lesson 6: The verb comes second

In a statement the verb is **always second**. Put something else first, and the subject moves after the verb.

```
Tyr kjold-er mur-in i nim-in.          "Tyr guards the wall at night."
I nim-in kjold-er Tyr mur-in.          "At night, Tyr guards the wall."
```

**Questions:**

- **Yes/no:** the verb comes first. *Kjolder dy murin?* "Do you guard the wall?"
- **Question words** start with *ku-* (*kum* who, *kut* what, *kur* where, *kuna* when, *kuvi* why, *kuso* how). The question word comes first, then the verb: *Kur es Vadanna?*

**Exercises**

6.1 Put *i frimin* "in the morning" first: *Astrid kjaler aul i frimin.*

6.2 Make a yes/no question: *Astrid kjaler aul.*

6.3 Translate: *Kum sundade? Kut sajer dy?*

## Lesson 7: No, don't, and may it be

| Pattern | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Not | *ne* goes right after the verb | *Em kjaler ne aul.* "I don't drink ale." |
| Command | the bare stem | *Kjold murin!* "Guard the wall!" |
| Don't | *ne* + stem | *Ne gan inn virin!* "Don't go into the forest!" |
| Blessing or wish | *ma* + who + infinitive | *Ma Lysmar gleida du.* "May Lysmar bless you." |

Norsund has no "please"; *ma* does that job: *Ma dy jova om aul.* "Some ale, please." (lit. "may you give me ale").

**Exercises**

7.1 Translate the gate inscription of the Kjoldmur. What comes first, and why does *run* come after the verb?

```
Fjol star-in lys-er run ne.
under star-DEF shine-PRS rune NEG
```

7.2 Translate into Norsund: *Don't flee! Stand with us (hjo omen)! May the stars keep you (kjolda du).*

7.3 Read the toast and translate it: *Kjal, thiolen! Ma staren kjolda dun.*

## Lesson 8: Whose, and how many

**Whose.** The owner comes first and takes **-i**, or **-s** after a vowel: *Tyri kyr* "Tyr's blade", *Gadrulli kjel* "Gadrull's pot". The owned thing takes no "the".

**Numbers**

| | | | | | | | | | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 yn | 2 dov | 3 thrim | 4 ves | 5 thamm | 6 sesk | 7 sev | 8 ath | 9 nyv | 10 tamm |

*Thamm* also means "hand". A number takes a plural noun, except *yn*: *dov kyrar* "two blades", *yn Vilkyr* "one free sword".

**Exercises**

8.1 Translate into Norsund: *two horses, three wolves, five blades, the free sword's blade.*

8.2 What is 17? And 70? (Hint: *tamm* + unit for the teens; unit + *tamm* for the tens.)

8.3 Auristar has 523,208 people. Read the number: *thammhaun dovtamm thrim mork, dovhaun ath*.

## Lesson 9: Will, have, can, must

| Meaning | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| will | *hal* + infinitive | *Em hal rida ve Auristar.* "I will ride to Auristar." |
| has done | *aga* (*ager*) + participle (-ad) | *Tyr ager vyngad.* "Tyr has won." |
| can, must, want | *orka, noda, thursa* + infinitive | *Em orker ne svafa.* "I can't sleep." |

**Exercises**

9.1 Translate: *Em noder gana. Sel thurser kjala.*

9.2 Translate into Norsund: *The Vilkyren will hunt the monster. I have found (hytta) the trail (slodin).*

9.3 Read this line from the Babel text: *Yt orker selen alkut smida.*

## Lesson 10: How do you know? And speaking like an Aurinay

End a sentence with a particle to say how you know it:

| Particle | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *sa* | I saw it | *Tyr valdade skrimmin, sa.* "Tyr slew the monster. (I saw it.)" |
| *sund* | so it's sung | *Skath nolver, sund.* "An enemy approaches. (So they say.)" |
| *stari* | so the stars say | *Auristar dul, stari.* "The Auristar fell. (It was fated.)" |

**In Auristar:**

- **Stars are living beings:** *staren* (not *starar*), and a star is *sel*.
- ***dyn* is polite "you"** for one person of rank.
- **A title gets *-a*** when you call someone: *Lysa!* "O Light!".
- **The accent** turns *th* into *s*.

**Exercises**

10.1 A Vilkyr reports a kill she saw herself. A bard repeats it in the next town. A priest says it was foretold. Add the right particle to *Tyr valdade skrimmin* for each.

10.2 Translate the Prophecy of the Auristar:

```
Thiark aur star ke ne vyrner.
Thal doler sel, thal hjald dyni heim.
Stari.
```

Why is the star *sel* and not *at*?

10.3 You meet the Third Light in Auristar. Greet them politely, and invite them to sit (*sjota*).

## Answer key

**Lesson 1**

- **1.1** THYOL-den, OW-ree-star, VIL-kü-ren, STAR-ee-rowg-haym, MEE-del-mar.
- **1.2** *Eth dy lys!*
- **1.3** *Mund!* (thanks) or *Kjal!* (cheers). Leaving: *Starkjold!*

**Lesson 2**

- **2.1** *ulfin, ulfen, ulfene*; *murin, murar, murarne*.
- **2.2** the horse; stars; the free swords; the homes.
- **2.3** *get*, *getene*.

**Lesson 3**

- **3.1** *Kyrarne es skarp. Hestin es thrakug. Starin es aur.*
- **3.2** The ale was warm.
- **3.3** *thiolin*, *thiolen* (*thiolene* = the brave ones).

**Lesson 4**

- **4.1** I am tired. You are brave. She (he, they) was hungry.
- **4.2** *Emi brod es Vilkyr. Seli hest es kvit. Dyni heim es fjelg.*
- **4.3** *Em sajer sal. Sel sajer om.*

**Lesson 5**

- **5.1** *Kjaladade* is wrong: the past of *kjala* is ***kjalade*** (*kjal-* + *-ade*). "Astrid drank ale."
- **5.2** *Tyr thiarkade skrimmin. Haakon nurade lydene. Ulfin beit gatin.* (*bita* is a strong verb, so not ~~*bitade*~~.)
- **5.3** I went to the tavern. Vadanna came at night.

**Lesson 6**

- **6.1** *I frimin kjaler Astrid aul.*
- **6.2** *Kjaler Astrid aul?*
- **6.3** Who sang? What do you see?

**Lesson 7**

- **7.1** "Beneath the star, no rune shines." The phrase *fjol starin* "beneath the star" comes first, so the verb *lyser* stays second and the subject *run* follows it; *ne* follows the subject. A shining rune is a charged, magical one, which Auristar forbids.
- **7.2** *Ne vysk! Stim hjo omen! Ma staren kjolda du.* (In a village: *Ma starar kjolda du.*)
- **7.3** "Drink, brave ones! May the stars keep you." (*dun* is "you" plural, object form.)

**Lesson 8**

- **8.1** *dov hesten* (horses are animate, so -en, not ~~-ar~~), *thrim ulfen*, *thamm kyrar*, *Vilkyrini kyr* (or *Vilkyri kyr* "a free sword's blade").
- **8.2** 17 = *tammsev*; 70 = *sevtamm*.
- **8.3** five hundred twenty-three thousand, two hundred eight.

**Lesson 9**

- **9.1** I must go. She wants to drink.
- **9.2** *Vilkyrene hal thiarka skrimmin. Em ager hyttad slodin.*
- **9.3** "Now they can make anything."

**Lesson 10**

- **10.1** The Vilkyr: *Tyr valdade skrimmin, sa.* The bard: *…, sund.* The priest: *…, stari.*
- **10.2** "Seek a silver star that does not move. Where it falls, there raise your home. So the stars say." In temple speech stars are living beings, so the star is *sel* "she/he", not *at* "it".
- **10.3** *Lys dyn, Lysa!* or *Lysa, velk!* Then *Ma dyn sjota.* "Please be seated." (Use *dyn*, never *dy*, to a Light.)
