Norsund

The common tongue of Thiolden, built in full: all three phases are done. It has Norse bones and a Kedre layer like English after 1066. The Auristar accent adds a Venetian softness, and the whole language is obsessed with stars. Every name already in canon decodes into it, and everything here lives in the lore repo with tools to translate, write, check and teach it.

About the concept image: it never reached the build, and the lore lead decided that Norsund 1.0 stands without it (D1). The language is built from the words already in records/.

What the research says

I covered five research tracks: construction method, historical sound change and language flavor, vocabulary and naming, writing systems, and how film/TV/game languages were built and governed. These nine rules came out of them, and each one shapes a choice below. The full report is in docs/languages/research/conlang-craft-report.md.

1 · Build from the names you already have

Every famous screen language grew out of a few words already on the page. High Valyrian came from two sentences and a handful of nouns, Dothraki from about 50 book words, and Klingon from a few lines in the first film.

→ For us: decode the canon words; don't rename them.

Peterson, dothraki.com; Okrand interviews

2 · Start with a naming language

A "naming language" is a sound system plus rules for compounding. With about 150 meaningful elements you can generate millions of consistent names. That's the layer a community of writers needs first.

→ For us: Phase 1 is the name-building kit.

Henning 1995, Fiat Lingua

3 · Get the Norse feel from how the grammar works

Peterson warns against taking a real language and changing bits of it ("Englerika"). The feel should come from processes: vowel changes (umlaut), the verb in second position (V2), a definite article on the end of the noun, verbs that change vowel for the past, and kennings.

→ For us: new roots are invented; Germanic look only where canon already has it.

Peterson, Fiat Lingua FL-00005A

4 · Give the language a history

Build an ancestor language, apply ordered regular sound changes, and let irregular forms come out of that history. Big cities change their speech fastest: Peterson made Meereen the most divergent dialect.

→ For us: Proto → Old Norsund → Norsund, with Auristar as the innovator.

Rosenfelder, LCK; Eldamo; Peterson

5 · Let the culture carve the meanings

Whatever a culture cares about gets short, efficient words. Splitting and merging meanings differently from English is the cure for "English with new words".

→ For us: stars count as living beings in the grammar; a tavern cook is called "fire".

Frommer; Rosenfelder; Nautilus

6 · Keep the spelling boring

Spelling should be "as uncreative as humanly possible", and apostrophes are "an abomination" unless they stand for a real sound such as a glottal stop.

→ For us: keyboard letters only, no accents, no apostrophes in Norsund.

Peterson, FL-00005A

7 · Shape the script to its medium

The writing tool shapes the letters: runes were cut in wood and Skyrim's dragon script was "carved with a talon". Tolkien's Cirth survived only for carving once Tengwar took over. Avoid one-for-one Latin letter swaps.

→ For us: runes for wood and old magic, a star script for Auristar's stone and crystal.

Bethesda; Cirth/Tengwar; neography.info

8 · Govern it like a living language

The models that work have a small fixed core plus controlled growth (Esperanto), tiers (Toki Pona's Linku survey), and batch releases (the Klingon Language Institute, Frommer's Na'vi blog). Whether a language can be copyrighted is unsettled: the Axanar court declined to rule.

→ For us: versioned core, PR-based growth, an open licence.

Linku; KLI; conlang.org/axanar

9 · Ship a kit with the language

Actors get the English line, the conlang line, a phonetic respelling, and audio at three speeds. Peterson's 4-page High Valyrian pronunciation guide pairs each letter with an English word and a "never like…" warning.

→ For us: a pronunciation sheet and naming guide alongside v1.0.

dedalvs.com HV guide

Decisions

These were lore calls, not linguistics. All ten are settled for 1.0 and recorded in records/languages/norsund/norsund.yaml.

  • D1

    Re-share the concept image so it can be reconciled.

    Decided by the lore lead: 1.0 stands as built, without the image.

  • D2

    How "Auristar" is said.

    OW-ree-star; au is always as in "cow".

  • D3

    Myr & Heid and Ur & Nett.

    The same pair: Myr and Heid are the Wandering Houses' old names, Ur and Nett the temple names.

  • D4

    Are stars grammatically alive in Auristar?

    Yes: Starisund says staren and uses sel for a star; country speech says starar.

  • D5

    Runes in Auristar.

    Allowed as plain, non-glowing writing; only rune magic is banned. Lysrit is the sacred and civic script beside them.

  • D6

    Is aur native or Kedre?

    Native, so Auristar is a fully native name.

  • D7

    Spelling drift in canon.

    Standard spellings applied across the lore records: Midelmar, Haakon, Volggan, Vairean, Rechtar (ORTH-06). varian-empire.yaml and vloggan.yaml were renamed to match.

  • D8

    Names that collide with other works.

    Renamed in the lore records: Valinor to Valnor ("north Valia"), Worgenkin to Ulfkin, Exeter to Exaire, Arbury to Arbaire (NAME-09).

  • D9

    Licence and steward.

    Language CC BY 4.0, fonts OFL, tools MIT. A Language Steward appointed by the lore lead reviews words before the vote.

  • D10

    Do Kedre and Norsund share an ancestor?

    Yes: Proto-Valian. Lysmar and Luxis are true cognates, and Grimm-style shifts separate the two branches.

Identity & history

Norsund means "north-tongue" (nor "north" + sund "voice, song, tongue"). Its history mirrors English after 1066: a Germanic language survives a Romance-speaking conqueror and keeps a lasting layer of the conqueror's vocabulary.

deep past · Farheim

Proto-Norsund

Reconstructed only. Starred forms such as *kuri. Its sound changes produced the y-vowels and short roots.

450 b.v.e → c. 100 a.v.e

Old Norsund

The settlers' tongue, written on rune slates. Canon: Vilkyr means "free sword" in Old Norsund. Later kept for oaths, law and rune-craft.

c. 100 a.v.e → now

Norsund

Common speech of the Vilnayan towns and the language most Thiolden races share.

90 a.v.e → now · Auristar

Starisund

"Star-tongue": Auristar's prestige dialect. As the biggest city it innovates fastest: living stars, Kedre trade words, softened sounds.

imperial · Keras

Kedre

The Vairean Empire's Latin-looking language (Luxis = Norsund Lysmar). It arrived with the conquest of 37–51 a.v.e and Doir Vaire's rule over the Vilnayan towns.

The design in one paragraph

Norsund's native layer covers everyday life, war, nature and family. It is Norse-feeling: short roots, clusters like kj-, thj- and fj-, the y-vowel, and stress on the first syllable. The Kedre layer covers trade, law, stone masonry and canals (mur "wall", rie "canal", merk "market"), the way Norman French gave English "beef", "court" and "castle". In Auristar, the Starisund accent shortens double consonants, softens final -n and turns th into s, much as Venetian softens Italian. Say "Thiolden" and you've told everyone where you grew up. That gives "Viking Venice" in sound as well as in stone.

Patterns already hidden in canon: -en is always a plural of persons (Vilkyren, Sundwanderen, Runmyren, Virnayen). The demonym always takes the first element plus -nay. Compounds always put the modifier first. So the community has been writing a head-final Germanic language without a grammar, and the grammar below formalises what was already there.

Still open: Dethiol (possibly Old Norsund *dauth-thiol, "brave unto death", which suits "the Undying"), Naum, Lindorin, Alkregg, Loogandril. Inherited Farheim names (Astrid, Haakon, Ulf, Tyr) can keep in-world meanings where they help: ulf "wolf".

Stress goes on the first syllable, always, and in compounds on the first element: THYOL-den, VIL-kyr-en, STAR-i-fjol-den. Kedre loans that keep two syllables keep Kedre's second-to-last stress, so you can hear the loan layer.

Syllables are (s)(C)(C)V(C)(C). Allowed onsets: single consonants; C+j (kj, fj, thj, hj, sj); C+r/l (br, dr, gr, kr, tr, thr, fl, kl, sl); s+stop (sk, st, sp, str, skj); sw, dw, kw. Common codas: -r, -l, -n, -nd, -ld, -rd, -rk, -st, -sk and double consonants. An unstressed i before a vowel is a glide: Thiol- = /θjol/.

Grammar

The essentials. The complete grammar (126 rules) is in grammar/ and docs/languages/norsund/reference-grammar.md. Glosses follow the Leipzig rules.

Nouns: living things vs objects

Every noun is animate (people, beasts, gods) or inanimate. Animacy decides the plural, and that's why -en only ever appears on people in canon.

SingularPlural"the" singular"the" plural
animate (Vilkyr, a free sword)VilkyrVilkyrenVilkyrinVilkyrene
inanimate (kyr, a blade)kyrkyrarkyrinkyrarne
after a vowel (nay, folk)naynayennaynnayene
  • The same root can switch class: kyr "blade" → kyrar, but Vilkyr "free-sword (person)" → Vilkyren.
  • "The" is a suffix (-in), as in Scandinavian. There is no word for "a": a bare noun is indefinite.
  • Possession uses -i (after a vowel, -s), placed before the owned thing: Tyri kyr "Tyr's blade". The same -i links compounds: Stari-fjolden, Aur-i-star.
  • Nouns have no other cases. Old Norsund had four, and they survive only in set phrases.
  • Theology in the grammar (D4): Starisund treats star as animate (staren); country speech still says starar.

Adjectives

Adjectives go before the noun and don't change form: aur star "silver star". Used as a noun, an adjective takes the people-plural: thiolen "the brave ones". Superlative -ast: thiolast "bravest" (Auristar folk etymology: "starred"). Adjectives from nouns take -a: Vilnaya "of the free folk".

Pronouns

There's no he or she: the third person marks only animate vs inanimate, which suits a world of Vulkin, Ulfkin, Dragonkin and humans. The object forms change the vowel, an old Germanic-style alternation.

Evidentials: how do you know?

An optional word at the end of the sentence says where the knowledge came from. It's required in oaths, prophecy and bardic tellings. Each one grew out of an ordinary word, which is how real grammar forms.

Word order

Main clauses put the verb second (V2). Front anything, and the subject moves after the verb. Yes/no questions start with the verb. Relative clauses take ke ("that, which, who"), a Kedre loan, just as English took "because" from French.

Words & name-building

Contributors need this most: a kit of parts and seven rules, enough to name a new village, river or guild without a linguist.

Seven rules for coining names

  1. Modifier first, head last. Thiol-den, Nim-kjold, Kjold-mur.
  2. Link with -i- when the first part owns the second: Stari-rauggheim "the star's keeping-house".
  3. Demonym = first element + -nay: Thiolden → Thiolnay, Auristar → Aurinay, Oudeheim → Oudenay, Midelmar → Midelnay.
  4. Plural: -en for people, -ar for things.
  5. Stress the first syllable, and keep names to three syllables or fewer when you can.
  6. Only these letters: a b d e f g h i j k l m n o p r s t u v w y and th kj gj ng.
  7. No apostrophes in Norsund. They belong to other races' languages, and each of those must say what sound the apostrophe marks.

Proto → Old1. Umlaut: u → y before i in the next syllable*kuri → *kyri

The order matters. Umlaut (rule 1) has to happen before the -i that triggered it disappears (rule 2). That's why kyr has a y with no i left in sight: the kind of fossil that makes a language feel real. The full history in grammar/13-history.yaml starts in Proto-Valian with three Grimm-style consonant shifts, Norse-style breaking (*telu → thiol) and umlaut. The engine runs it and its tests check all 31 cognate sets.

Kedre, the conquerors' layer

Kedre has a recognisable shape: vowel-final words, stress on the second-to-last syllable, no th or y, endings like -is, -as, -aire (Luxis, Keras, Reinovaire). When Norsund borrows a word it drops the final vowel and moves stress to the front: Kedre murra → mur, rivia → rie, merkadu → merk, ke → ke. Kedre and Proto-Norsund descend from one deep ancestor, Proto-Valian, the way Latin and Germanic descend from Proto-Indo-European, so Luxis and lys are true cognates (D10). The complete grammar, dictionary and texts are in records/languages/kedre/.

Runes & Lysrit

There are two scripts with two different jobs, as in Tolkien's world, where Cirth carried on for carving after Tengwar took over everyday writing. Confirmed on 19 Nov: runes may appear in Auristar as plain writing that never glows; only rune magic is banned.

Runes · runar

Old Norsund's everyday script: staves cut into wood, bone and slate, still used across Thiolden, Auristar included. Canon says the runes are "just the written word of the people alive at that time". They become magic only when charged with aether, and a charged rune glows. That charging is what raised the dead at Marheim, and it's what Auristar forbids. Every rune is also a Norsund word. A short-twig form is used on wood, a long-branch form on stone. Merfolk runes are a separate tradition. Not designed yet; screen every design against hate-symbol databases.

Lysrit · "light-writing"

Auristar's sacred and civic script, proposed as an invention of Starlight Library scholars after the First Ring was built (c. 100–124 a.v.e). It's used on temples, the Kjoldmur, the Lights' decrees and anything holy. Because it was designed rather than evolved, it can be systematic, as Hangul is. Each letter is drawn on the seven-point grid of Auristar's emblem, top point up. Consonants are straight strokes between the seven points. Vowels are stars placed on a point, laid out like a vowel chart: front vowels on the right, back vowels on the left, low vowels at the bottom. The emblem's cyan heart is lit for voiced sounds. A violet seven-pointed star before a word marks a sacred name.

Lysrit fits the visual brief: inlaid crystal studs at each point and carved lines in white stone. At night, lit windows could spell words across a tower face. It also works as an embroidery or laser-cut pattern on techwear, and as crisp vector shapes in game interfaces.

Concept letter sheet

Sounds made in the same part of the mouth share a base bar: lips use the top bar, the tooth ridge the middle bar, the back of the mouth the bottom bar. Extra strokes give the manner of the sound, and a dot at the centre means voiced. ə is the unstressed e of endings. The script makes that distinction, which the romanization leaves out. The shapes are final. Treatments for carved stone, stained glass, UI, wood, slate and embroidery are in writing-systems.md. The full sheet, with digits, the long-hundred sign and evidential sigils, is in the workbench's Write tab.

How a new word becomes canon

  1. Propose by pull request: one YAML entry with meaning, parts, and the story or product that needs it.
  2. Check automatically in CI: allowed letters, syllable rules, no clash with an existing word, no real-world slur or brand in any major language.
  3. Review: a language steward (D9) checks fit and suggests the better form, as Frommer does for Na'vi.
  4. Ratify by the existing contributor vote, released in batches (KLI-style word releases).
  5. Tier it: core words change only by supermajority; common words grow freely; a sandbox lets products use unratified words if they're labelled.
  6. Version it: "official" means the product passes the checker against a named canon version, e.g. Norsund 1.0. Mistakes already shipped become recorded variants and are never silently erased, as Okrand treated on-screen Klingon.
